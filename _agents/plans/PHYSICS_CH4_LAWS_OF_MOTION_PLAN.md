## 1. Why 17 pages, and why the split lands where it does

NCERT's own chapter is short on scenario variety — it introduces Newton's three laws, common forces, and circular motion, but only two of its worked examples (4.15, 4.16) touch connected bodies, and it never shows a pulley-constraint derivation, a wedge, or friction combined with an incline. **Every scenario the founder is asking for — the different friction situations, the different constrained-motion setups — has to come from the two reference books**, which is exactly what sources 2 and 3 are for.

Rather than one page titled "Friction" and one titled "Free Body Diagrams," each gets **split by scenario family**, because a single page cannot hold 5+ genuinely different worked examples at the step-solver depth the founder wants (4+ gated steps each) without becoming an unreadable wall. The split:

| Block | Tags covered | Pages | Why this many |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newton's Laws (foundations) | `tag_nlm_1` | 2 | 1st/3rd law + momentum is conceptually dense but scenario-light; 2nd law + equilibrium needs its own page for F=ma, impulse, and multi-force equilibrium problems. |
| **Free Body Diagrams & Constraint Motion** | `tag_nlm_2`, `tag_nlm_4` | **4** | Single-body FBDs (block on floor/incline/wall/hanging mass) is one distinct skill; connected bodies sharing one acceleration (string, incline, stacked blocks) is a second; pulley constraint equations (fixed pulley / Atwood, Type 1) is a third, with enough worked variety (unequal masses, three-body chains) to fill a page alone; movable pulleys + multi-pulley systems + wedge constraints (Type 2) is a fourth, and is the single hardest skill in the whole chapter. Cramming these into one or two pages was the exact failure mode the founder flagged. |
| **Friction** | `tag_nlm_3` | **3** | Basics (static vs kinetic, angle of friction, angle of repose) is the concept page; pulling/pushing at an angle + stacked-block friction is a second distinct scenario family; walls/wedges/rough-incline/minimum-speed problems is a third — each of these is a genuinely different force diagram, not a formula substitution. |
| Pseudo Force & Non-Inertial Frames | `tag_nlm_5` | 2 | The trick itself (why you add `−ma_frame`) plus lift/elevator problems is one page; applying it to blocks-on-accelerating-wedges and pendulums-in-vehicles (harder, and ties back into the constraint-motion pages) is a second. |
| Circular Dynamics | `tag_nlm_6` | 2 | Vertical circle + conical pendulum (force sources: tension/gravity) is one page; banking of roads (with and without friction, safe-speed range) is a second, since banking is a distinct enough setup (component resolution on a banked surface) to earn its own worked examples rather than being a subsection. |
| Opener + close-out | all | 4 | 1 chapter opener (`p0`) + recap/mind-map (`p14`) + NCERT exercises verbatim (`p15`) + JEE/NEET drill (`p16`), matching the Ch.2/Ch.3 pattern. |

**13 content pages + 1 opener + 3 close-out = 17 total.** This is one page fewer than Ch.3's 18, but meaningfully denser per page — every content page here is built around 2–4 distinct worked scenarios rather than one continuous derivation, because that is what "multiple scenarios per topic, each with worked examples" requires structurally.

## 2. What "more visuals and interactions, deferred sims" means concretely for this build

- **Every content page gets 2 hand-drawn SVG figures, not 1** (Ch.3's average was ~1 per page with an image block). Constraint-motion and friction pages in particular need a "before" FBD figure and a "the trick" figure (e.g., p6 needs one figure showing the movable-pulley setup and a second showing the tension-doubling free-body isolation) — this is the visual density the founder is asking for, delivered without a sim.
- **Step-solvers are the primary vehicle for "why a formula alone won't answer it"** — per scenario, not per page: a friction page with 3 scenario families gets 3+ step-solvers, each opening on the free-body diagram (which forces get drawn, which don't) before any equation, exactly like Ch.3's in-situ vector-resolution rule (§1 of the Ch.3 plan) — a constraint or friction diagram is "revised in place" the first time each scenario appears on its page, never assumed.
- **Sim candidates are flagged, not built, in this pass.** Marked inline in §3 with 🎮. The two strongest candidates:
  - A **pulley/constraint sandbox** (drag masses, see the string-length constraint update live) for p5/p6 — this is the single place in the whole Ch.0–Ch.4 arc where a static figure most under-serves the concept, because constraint equations are fundamentally about *relative motion*, which a still image can't show.
  - A **friction force-vs-applied-force graph you build by pulling** (static friction rising 1:1 with applied force up to `μ_s N`, then dropping to `μ_k N`) for p7 — this is exactly the kind of "formula alone won't give you the answer" idea (self-adjusting static friction) that a direct-manipulation board teaches better than a paragraph.
  - Both are noted for the founder to greenlight in the sim pass; **neither blocks building the 17 pages now.**
